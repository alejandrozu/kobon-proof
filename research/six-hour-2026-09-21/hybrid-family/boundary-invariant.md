# A boundary invariant for the eleven-line hybrid family

Working proof, 22 September 2026. Author: Alejandro Zarzuelo Urdiales, with computational assistance. This note strengthens the earlier even-family consequence by one triangle at every member. The proof uses the published BBL geometric doubling theorem and a rational interval certificate for its 21-line boundary input. It is not yet an end-to-end Lean proof, and priority remains to be checked.

## Statement

Let `q = 10 * 2^t`, with `t >= 0`. There is a simple straight-line arrangement of `q+2` lines containing at least

\[
\boxed{\frac{q^2-4}{3}+\frac q2}
\]

bounded triangular cells. Equivalently, for `M = q+2`,

\[
K_s(M)\ge \frac{M(M-5/2)}3-1.
\]

Thus these even orders are within one triangle of Blanc's polynomial upper bound **for simple arrangements**. That upper bound is not asserted for the unrestricted classical Kobon problem. The old infinite guarantee subtracted two; the finite counts at 12, 22, 42, 82 and 162 already had this stronger value. The new content is the all-parameter boundary argument, not those finite counts and not BBL doubling itself.

The underlying odd arrangement is the established family with `q+1` lines and `(q^2-4)/3` triangles. Its equations, seed and attribution are in `research/kobon-hybrid/manuscript.md`.

## Definitions and the induction invariant

A right ray on a nonvertical line `y=m(x-a)` is the half-line from its largest-x intersection vertex in the direction `(1,m)`. If two lines share their largest-x vertex, their two right rays bound an unbounded wedge cell. They are called a right wedge. More generally a wedge is visible from a normal `w` when both its boundary rays have positive scalar product with `w`.

Keep the normal

\[
w=(10,-13).
\]

At the `q+1`-line stage, we prove:

1. At least `q/2` wedges are visible from `w`.
2. For every stage from `q=20` onward, the line `R` whose intercept on `Y_0:y=0` is largest has slope `0<m_R<10/13`; its rightmost vertex is its intersection with `Y_0`.

The initial eleven-line seed has five visible wedges, now proved in `Kobon.HybridBoundary.seed_visible` for the full real-parameter family. There are two independently checked choices for the 21-line base. `verify_boundary_base.py` directly certifies ten specified visible pairs for the original BBL coefficients for **every** `0<epsilon<=10^-5`. It also checks that `w` is parallel to none of the 21 lines. These are 380 strict endpoint sign checks. Each homogeneous evaluation is affine in epsilon, so two endpoints suffice. Rational intervals enclose every trigonometric coefficient, and no floating-point sign is used.

Alternatively, the new `Kobon.BBLSeed21` module uses fixed rational approximations to the ten added slopes and **actual**, unapproximated tangent-grid intercepts. Lean verifies at least 132 triangles, all 19 distinguished triangles, ten visible pairs, simplicity, and arbitrarily small positive parameters. This base may be used directly before applying BBL at `q=20`; it gives the same family lower bounds. The slopes and finite certificates are generated by `generate_seed21.py`. The finite box checks use `native_decide`; the trigonometric enclosures and the geometric soundness proof use only standard Lean axioms.

The rightmost line at that stage is the line with `beta=pi/2-pi/20`. Its slope is positive and below `10/13`. For the original BBL coefficients, all its intersections except with `Y_0` have x-coordinate less than `cot(pi/10)+10^-2`, whereas its intercept is `cot(pi/20)`. For the rational-slope replacement base, `Kobon.BBLSeed21.rightmost_last` directly proves that every intersection on this line has x-coordinate at most its intersection with `Y_0`; the slope inequalities are also Lean theorems. This proves invariant 2 at the base. The same observation below proves its preservation.

## One doubling, for `q` divisible by four and `q>=20`

Write `alpha=pi/q`, `a=cot(alpha)`, `delta=q^-6` and `kappa=m_min/q^10`. BBL adds the `q` lines

\[
M_\beta:y=\mu_\beta(x-\tan\beta),\qquad
\mu_\beta=\kappa(\sin(2\beta)+\delta\cot\beta),
\]

where `beta` ranges through the odd half-multiples of `alpha` in `(-pi/2,pi/2)`. The exceptional old lines meet above `Y_0`, so BBL's sign is positive at every stage. BBL proves `|mu_beta|<2*m_min/q^10`. Hence all added lines have right rays visible from `w`; all of their slopes have absolute value below `10/13`.

We use the following estimates proved in BBL Proposition 3.1, equations (5)--(9):

- Every old--new intersection has x-coordinate within `3/q^9` of that old line's intercept, and its absolute y-coordinate is less than `3*m_min/q^9`.
- Every old--old vertex other than the exceptional pair has absolute y-coordinate greater than `m_min/q`.
- A new pair with `beta+gamma=-pi/2` meets at x-coordinate greater than `q^5`; one with sum `pi/2` meets at x-coordinate less than `-q^5`.
- Every other new--new intersection is within `1/q^2` of `tan(beta+gamma)`. Its displacement has the sign of `(beta+gamma)*beta*gamma`, and its nonzero absolute displacement exceeds `3/q^8`.

The fourth item excludes sum zero, where the intersection has x-coordinate exactly zero. All uses below respect that exception.

### Old visible wedges survive, except possibly the one on the right of `Y_0`

Every old free ray whose endpoint is not on `Y_0` stays in the open half-plane containing that endpoint, because it cannot cross the old line `Y_0` again. Except at the exceptional pair, its endpoint lies outside the strip `|y|<=m_min/q`, whereas every new intersection with its supporting old line lies inside the much narrower strip `|y|<3*m_min/q^9`. Hence no new line meets that ray.

The exceptional pair meets above `Y_0`. Any free ray starting there must point into the upper half-plane. Each new line meets either exceptional old line below `Y_0`: its intercept lies between the two nearest nonexceptional grid intercepts, `mu_beta` has the sign of `beta`, and `|mu_beta|` is smaller than either exceptional old slope. The equation for that intersection gives the claimed negative y-coordinate. Those rays therefore survive as well.

A line missing both rays of an old wedge cannot enter its open pointed cone: either end of a line inside such a cone would have to cross its boundary. Thus old wedges away from `Y_0` persist. A visible wedge touching `Y_0` must use the right ray of `Y_0`, since `w dot (-1,0)<0`. At most one visible old wedge is consequently lost.

### There are `q/2+1` new right wedges

There are four disjoint classes.

**A. Negative complementary pairs.** Pair negative angles with sum `-pi/2`. Because `q` is divisible by four, there is no angle exactly `-pi/4`. These yield `q/4` pairs. Their intersections have x-coordinate greater than `q^5`; all other intersections on either supporting line have smaller x-coordinate. Thus each pair is a right wedge.

**B. Positive almost-complementary pairs.** Pair distinct positive angles with sum `pi/2-alpha`. This yields `q/4-1` pairs: the self-pair at

\[
\beta_* = \pi/4-\alpha/2
\]

is excluded, as is the largest positive angle. Each such vertex lies at x-coordinate greater than `a+3/q^8`. Its old-line intersections have x-coordinate at most `a+3/q^9`; every other finite new--new intersection on either line has x-coordinate at most `cot(2alpha)+1/q^2`, or is the far negative complementary intersection. The other possible branch producing a positive value near `cot(alpha)`, namely a sum `-pi/2-alpha`, cannot occur here: either supporting angle is positive and the other angle exceeds `-pi/2`, so their sum exceeds `-pi/2`. Its intersection with `Y_0` has x-coordinate below `a`. Thus these are right wedges too.

**C. The central positive new line and the rightmost old line `R`.** We prove that `M_(beta_*)` and `R` have the largest-x intersection on both lines. Put `m=m_R` and `u=kappa/m`; thus `0<u<=q^-10`. For any new angle `beta`, write

\[
g_\beta=\sin(2\beta)+\delta\cot\beta,\qquad
h_\beta=g_\beta(a-\tan\beta).
\]

The old--new intersection on `R` has x-coordinate

\[
a+u\,\frac{h_\beta}{1-u g_\beta}.
\]

Its leading term is

\[
H_\beta=a\sin(2\beta)+\cos(2\beta)-1
=\frac{\sin(2\beta+\alpha)}{\sin\alpha}-1.
\]

On the discrete grid, `H_beta` has a unique maximum at `beta_*`. For every other beta,

\[
H_{\beta_*}-H_\beta\ge 2\sin\alpha\ge4/q.
\]

The perturbation satisfies `|h_beta-H_beta|<2/q^4`, because `|a|<q` and `|cot(beta)|<q`. BBL gives `|g_beta|<2`. We may use `|h_beta|<3q` and `|u*g_beta|<1/2`, whence

\[
\left|\frac{h_\beta}{1-u g_\beta}-h_\beta\right|<12/q^9.
\]

Therefore the corrected difference is positive, since

\[
4/q-4/q^4-24/q^9>0\qquad(q\ge20).
\]

This proves the unique largest old--new intersection on `R`. It is to the right of `a`, because `mu_(beta_*)>0` and `a>tan(beta_*)`. All old vertices on `R` are at x-coordinate at most `a` by the induction invariant.

On `M_(beta_*)`, the potential near-`a` new partner is precisely its excluded self-pair. All other finite new--new intersections are at most `cot(2alpha)+1/q^2`, and its complementary partner meets far to the left. Every old line other than `R` has intercept at most `cot(2alpha)` (or an exceptional intercept near zero), with old--new displacement below `3/q^9`. Thus the intersection with `R` is rightmost on this line also. Both right rays are visible from `w`.

**D. The rightmost new line and `Y_0`.** Let `beta_max=pi/2-alpha/2`. Its intercept is `cot(alpha/2)`. Every other vertex on this line is strictly to the left: old--new vertices are at most `a+3/q^9`, and its remaining new--new vertices are at most `a+1/q^2` or far negative. The elementary inequality

\[
\cot(\alpha/2)>\cot\alpha+1/q^2
\]

holds for `q>=20`. This gives one more right wedge, and proves invariant 2 for the next stage.

For the separation used in B and C, it suffices that

\[
\cot\alpha-\cot(2\alpha)=1/\sin(2\alpha)>1>1/q^2+3/q^9.
\]

The four classes total

\[
q/4+(q/4-1)+1+1=q/2+1.
\]

They all involve a newly added line, so none is an old surviving wedge. Losing at most one old visible wedge proves

\[
c_{2q+1}(w)\ge c_{q+1}(w)+q/2.
\]

Starting with ten visible wedges at 21 lines gives at least `q/2` at every subsequent `q+1`-line stage. The eleven-line base has five as above.

For any prescribed finite target `t`, choose

\[
0<\varepsilon\le\min\{10^{-5},1/(40\cdot2^t)\}.
\]

The uniform base certificates hold at this parameter, and BBL's exceptional-intercept smallness requirement holds at every stage needed for that target. The parameter is allowed to depend on `t`; one fixed positive epsilon is not asserted to permit infinitely many doublings.

## Closing the wedges

Choose a line `w dot (x,y)=H` beyond every old vertex. It misses all old bounded cells and closes each visible wedge to a distinct new triangle. It can be chosen nonparallel to every old line, and no old pair intersection lies on it. Thus simplicity is retained. This is the geometric content of the existing `Kobon.Exterior.extension` theorem once the visible pairs are provided.

The odd family has `(q^2-4)/3` triangles, and the added line contributes at least `q/2`. This proves the stated even-family inequality.

## Scope and remaining checks

- The finite base interval verifier is exact rational Python and requires no floating-point oracle. Its mathematical analytic input remains Machin's identity and the Taylor remainder theorem.
- The complete inductive geometry has been written out above, but is not formalized in Lean. The existing arithmetic family theorem and exterior-construction theorem do not themselves certify this new invariant.
- New formal components prove the seed visibility (`HybridBoundary`), both implications between extremal intersections and visibility and preservation behind a vertex (`BBLExtrema`), the discrete sine-grid gap and stability under rational perturbation (`BBLMaximization`), sharp actual tangent values (`BBLTangentBounds`), and the uniform 21-line base with its rightmost-line invariant (`BBLSeed21`). These are genuine geometric/analytic lemmas, but must not be described as the completed all-q arrangement theorem.
- The statement improves our previous infinite even guarantee by one, and extends already known finite witnesses within this project. It does not establish new finite records at the small checkpoint orders.
- The general odd-to-even recurrence for arbitrary optimal arrangements does not follow. Only the specified BBL family carries this invariant.

References: [BBL (2007), Proposition 3.1 and Remark 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/0706.0723v1); [Blanc (2008), simple-affine upper bounds](https://arxiv.org/html/0801.2845v2). The odd compatible seed and its prior-work attribution remain as recorded in the main hybrid manuscript.
